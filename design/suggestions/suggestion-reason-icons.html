Four things this has to answer

None of these kill the idea, but each needs a decision before it is built.

One reason carries a number

“Holding above your last set — you had 2 reps left in it” is the only sentence with data in it, and no glyph carries a 2. Either that one signal keeps a micro-label (↑ 2 left), or the number is lost.

Nobody is born knowing a glyph

A mark with no words is unreadable the first time. The three answers are the legend line, a tap that reveals the sentence, or teaching it once at first run and trusting it after.

VoiceOver keeps the sentence

The glyph is a visual shorthand, not a reduction in what the app knows. The row's accessibility label should still read the full sentence the engine produced.

The stale banner stays text

It names a date — “a workout from Mar 1” — and a mark cannot. It sits above the strips and is unaffected either way.

Tap to hear it in words

The recovery path for the two problems above: tapping a row's mark drops the original sentence in underneath it, and the micro-label rides along for the floor case.

Where I'd land

Signal tile, with tap to reveal. It is the version that actually does what you asked — the prose is gone, the strip is shorter, and one mark carries the reason. The tap keeps the sentence available for the people who want it without spending a single pixel on the people who do not, and it is the only answer that also fixes the floor case, since the revealed text can say "2 reps left" where a mark cannot.

If you would rather not rely on a tap, take Glyph and a word instead. It teaches itself forever and needs no interaction — you just do not get the height back. The legend line is the weaker middle: it costs space and still asks people to look away from the row they are reading.

What I would not do is colour the rails by signal. The rail says "this is a suggestion"; the mark says why. Colouring both makes three coloured bars fight the accent that the rest of the screen uses for the thing you are supposed to tap.
