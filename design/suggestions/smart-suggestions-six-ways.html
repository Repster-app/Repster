What's there today

Four observations that the alterations answer. All of them come out of the current layout, not from taste.

  • Three strips say almost the same thing

    Each pending set gets its own 58 pt strip with its own wand tile, its own rail and its own sentence — but sets 2 and 3 carry an identical prescription. The repetition is the loudest thing in the module.

  • The weight is the only target with no home in the row

    The reps field already shows the target as a placeholder and the RIR chip already shows a dimmed target. Weight shows "0" — so the one number the engine computes is the one number that got exiled to a separate section.

  • You read it here, you act on it there

    The module is read-only. Accepting a suggestion happens on the keypad's accent key, which already renders wand + 55 kg. The strips describe an action that lives one screen-layer away.

  • It grows with the set count

    At 58 pt per pending set, a five-set exercise puts roughly 300 pt of suggestion under a 300 pt table — the part you need between sets is the part you have to scroll to.

Five alterations

The first three keep the module where it is and change how it reads. The last two move the suggestion to where you act on it, and shrink or delete the section.

If it were mine

In-row ghosts and Next up are the pair. They fix different halves of the same problem: the ghost puts the number where the thumb already is, and the hero keeps one plain-English reason on screen so the module still explains itself. Together they cut the section from three strips to one card and a line.

Quiet ledger and Plan bar are alternatives to the hero, not additions — pick at most one. Ledger if you want it to disappear; Plan bar if you want the whole session legible at a glance.

Change framing is the one worth holding. It is the most useful of the five and the only one that needs new data — the reference load has to come back into the display model before the copy can be honest.
