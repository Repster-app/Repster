# Repster: first week of social content

Drafts prepared September 19, 2026. Nothing has been published.

## Audience and message to test

Start with iPhone lifters who follow their own routine, already record sets, and want their training history to help them choose what to do next. This is an initial hypothesis, not a validated customer segment.

Core message: **Your last workout should help you choose your next weight.**

Supporting description: Repster is an iPhone workout tracker that keeps previous performance close and suggests weights using your history and targets.

Use these three posts to learn which problem people recognize: finding previous performance, choosing a weight, or repeating a saved routine. Friends using Repster regularly are a useful source of language about these problems, but their free subscriptions do not establish willingness to pay.

Ask those friends, individually: “What did you use before Repster? What do you use most during a workout? What would you miss if you switched back?” Keep their actual words; do not turn them into public testimonials without permission.

## Simple production setup

- Record real app footage vertically. Use a clean demo workout with example data; label it “Example workout” where needed so it does not imply a real person's results.
- Capture three short sequences: viewing previous performance, viewing a weight suggestion, and opening a saved routine and starting it.
- Edit in a 1080 × 1920 project. Crop or zoom enough that the relevant numbers are readable on a phone. Keep text away from the top and bottom controls of social apps.
- Use the opening sentence immediately, then show the app. One idea per video, approximately 15–20 seconds.
- The first versions can use only footage and text. Voice and face footage are optional.
- Save one editing template with the same font, text position, and ending. Duplicate it for subsequent posts.
- Export a clean master video and upload it separately to Instagram Reels and TikTok. Adapt the same idea for X. Use Reddit for relevant conversations, with a separate draft below.

CapCut supports [text to speech](https://www.capcut.com/tools/text-to-speech) and [automatic captions with manual correction](https://www.capcut.com/help/auto-captions-in-capcut). Availability and paid features can vary. If using voice, check every generated caption against the actual words and app names.

## Post 1: remembering your previous workout

Purpose: test a familiar problem that requires little explanation.

| Time | Footage | On-screen text |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 0–3 seconds | Open an exercise in an active demo workout | “What did you lift last time?” |
| 3–8 seconds | Show the previous performance display; zoom in | “Your previous sets, while you train.” |
| 8–14 seconds | Return to the current sets and log an example set | “Check last time. Log this time.” |
| 14–18 seconds | Hold a readable app screen with Repster name | “Try Repster on iPhone. First 10 workouts free.” |

Instagram/TikTok caption:

> Remembering last week's sets shouldn't be part of today's workout. Repster keeps previous performance close while you log. Try your next workout on iPhone — your first 10 workouts are free. App Store link in bio.

Use “link in bio” only after adding a usable link. Otherwise replace the last sentence with “Search Repster on the App Store.”

X draft, with the clip attached:

> What did you lift last time? Repster puts previous performance alongside your workout so you can check it while logging. iPhone · first 10 workouts free. https://apps.apple.com/us/app/repster-gym-workout-tracker/id6766094305

## Post 2: choosing a weight

Purpose: test the product's more distinctive reason to try it.

| Time | Footage | On-screen text |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 0–3 seconds | Show the exercise and weight entry area | “Same weight today, or go heavier?” |
| 3–8 seconds | Show the actual previous performance for that exercise | “Start with what you lifted before.” |
| 8–14 seconds | Show a real weight suggestion and the target it relates to | “Repster suggests a weight from your history and targets.” |
| 14–19 seconds | Hold the suggestion screen; add a small Repster name | “You choose the weight. First 10 workouts free.” |

Keep the displayed history, targets, and suggestion consistent. Do not animate the suggestion changing unless the app actually changes it in the recorded flow. Do not describe a suggestion as the guaranteed optimal weight or promise faster gains.

Instagram/TikTok caption:

> Same weight, or a little more? Repster uses your workout history and targets to suggest a weight. You decide what to lift. Available on iPhone — first 10 workouts free. App Store link in bio.

X draft, with the clip attached:

> Same weight today, or go heavier? Repster uses your workout history and targets to suggest a weight for your set. You decide what to lift. First 10 workouts free on iPhone. https://apps.apple.com/us/app/repster-gym-workout-tracker/id6766094305

## Post 3: starting the routine you already follow

Purpose: test convenience for people who already know their training plan.

## Reddit: a separate conversation

Use only in a community or recurring feedback thread whose current rules permit founder/product discussions. Reddit's [spam guidance](https://support.reddithelp.com/hc/en-us/articles/360043504051-Spam) emphasizes authentic participation and community-specific rules. Tailor the post to that discussion; do not paste it across communities.

Title:

> What do you look at in your workout log before choosing today's weight?

Body:

> I'm building Repster, an iPhone workout tracker, and I'm trying to understand this part of logging better.
>
> When you repeat an exercise, do you check the last workout, your best recent set, or something else before choosing a weight? And do you actually want a suggested weight, or mainly easier access to your history?
>
> I'd appreciate specific examples of what you do between sets. Happy to explain how I've approached it if that's useful.

Answer replies yourself and use them to refine the message. AI can help summarize the themes afterward.

## Reusable AI writing prompt

Copy this and add a description of the actual recording:

> Help me turn this real app recording into a 15–20 second social video for Repster, an iPhone workout tracker. The audience is people who follow their own gym routine and already track sets. The current offer is the first 10 workouts free, then payment is needed to continue logging.
>
> Recording: [describe exactly what is visible and what actions happen].
>
> Give me five opening lines of at most eight words each, one timed shot list using only this footage, brief text overlays, an optional voiceover, one Instagram/TikTok caption, and one X post. Focus on one recognizable lifting problem. Use plain language. Do not invent features, founder experiences, testimonials, research claims, workout results, or guarantees. Do not describe the app as free forever. Identify any shot that requires a new recording.

## A manageable first week

1. Capture the three sequences and make Post 1. Allow extra time to learn the editor.
2. Publish Post 1 to Instagram and TikTok; adapt it for X. Read and answer relevant replies.
3. Duplicate the editing template to make Posts 2 and 3; publish them on separate days.
4. Participate in one relevant Reddit discussion where the rules allow it.
5. At the end of the week, record views, profile visits, useful replies, link activity, and attributed installs where available. Use first-workout completion and payment data as downstream signals when attribution supports it. Do not assume every new install came from a post.

These three posts are exploratory examples, not a controlled experiment. Look for people recognizing the problem and asking about the app. Repeat promising ideas with different openings before deciding they work or fail. With few users, a handful of views or a single purchase cannot establish a dependable conversion rate.

Keep the audience, offer, and app-store destination reasonably consistent while learning which message attracts relevant people. Change one major element at a time where practical.
